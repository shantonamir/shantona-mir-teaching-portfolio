Collaborative Curriculum Design Project

Overview

The English for Legal Purposes (ELP) Track was designed to prepare international students for successful participation in Brigham Young University's Master of Laws (LL.M.) program by developing the academic English skills required in U.S. legal education.

The project involved designing a complete English for Specific Purposes (ESP) curriculum, integrating language instruction with authentic legal contexts and law-school expectations.

Project Goals

The curriculum aims to help students

  • Develop legal English proficiency.
  • Strengthen academic reading and writing skills.
  • Improve oral communication in legal settings.
  • Prepare for law school classroom participation.
  • Build familiarity with U.S. legal discourse and academic culture.

Project Components

The curriculum includes

  • Student Needs Analysis
  • Learning Outcomes
  • Course Outcomes
  • Learning Experiences
  • Instructional Materials

  • Future Development Plan

My Contribution

As part of this collaborative curriculum design project, I contributed to the needs analysis and the development of the speaking component. I reviewed research and examples of existing English for Legal Purposes and legal English programs to understand how other institutions designed language curricula for law students. I identified relevant approaches, skills, and activities that could inform our curriculum.

The project team was divided into smaller groups focusing on areas such as speaking, listening, and needs analysis. Within my group, I helped develop ideas and recommendations for the speaking component based on the research we collected. We then combined the work from each group to create the complete English for Legal Purposes curriculum.

Highlights

Needs Analysis

Designed learner profiles based on the linguistic and academic demands of international law students entering an LL.M. program.

Curriculum Outcomes

Developed detailed learning outcomes across

  • Reading
  • Writing
  • Listening
  • Speaking

aligned with academic legal communication.

Learning Experiences

Designed authentic classroom activities including

  • Moot court simulations
  • Client interviews
  • Legal case discussions
  • Academic debates
  • Reading fluency activities
  • Timed legal writing
  • Source synthesis
  • Oral presentations

Materials

Curated authentic legal resources, judicial opinions, legal texts, novels, and case studies to support language development in legal contexts.

Future Development

The project concludes with recommendations for pilot implementation, teacher training, curriculum refinement, and additional needs analysis before full deployment.
